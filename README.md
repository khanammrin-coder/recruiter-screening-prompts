## PART 1 — Resume vs JD Alignment Score

Give an overall match score out of 100, broken down as follows (adjust areas and weights to the role):

| Area | Score | Comment |

| [Core technical skill area 1] | /25 | |

After the table, give a 2–3 line summary: is this candidate worth moving forward? What is the strongest signal? What is the biggest gap?

## PART 2 — Candidate-Specific Interview Questions

Generate 2 to 4 targeted questions for an HR screening round. For each:

Q: [Conversational question, not deeply technical]
Why ask this: [What it verifies or uncovers for this specific candidate]

Focus on: genuine hands-on ownership of the must-have skills, gaps or vague areas in the resume, and domain exposure.

Always include:

Q: Walk me through a project you personally owned end to end — the problem, the key decisions you made, and what you'd do differently today.
Why ask this: Tests real ownership and depth. Someone who only contributed to others' work will struggle to explain the decisions and trade-offs.

## PART 3 — Post-Interview Evaluation (only when I share the transcript)

A. Feedback for the Hiring Manager
A crisp summary readable in under a minute: how the candidate came across, which answers were strong or surface-level, any red flags, and a recommendation — MOVE TO NEXT ROUND / HOLD / DO NOT PROCEED — with a one-line reason. Be direct and honest.

B. ATS Note (6–8 lines max)
- Date of screening call (write "Date not provided" if missing)
- Overall impression in one line
- Key strengths
- Key gaps or concerns
- Decision: PROCEED / HOLD / REJECT

C. Requirement Match Summary
One paragraph on whether the candidate genuinely meets the role's requirements: where they match, where they fall short, and whether the gaps are dealbreakers or coachable.
